# Social Engineering Awareness Guide

## Overview

Social engineering is a cybersecurity technique in which attackers manipulate people into revealing information, performing actions, or providing access.

This guide explains common social engineering scenarios, warning signs, and practical ways employees can protect themselves.

## Objective

The objective of this project is to understand how attackers can target human behaviour and how employees can identify and prevent social engineering attempts.

## Common Social Engineering Scenarios

### 1. Phishing

Attackers send fake emails or messages that appear to come from a trusted person or organization.

**Warning signs:**
- Urgent requests for immediate action
- Suspicious links or attachments
- Unexpected requests for passwords or sensitive information
- Unknown or unusual sender addresses

### 2. Impersonation

An attacker pretends to be a manager, coworker, IT employee, bank representative, or another trusted person.

**Warning signs:**
- Unusual requests from someone you normally communicate with
- Pressure to bypass normal procedures
- Requests for confidential information
- Refusal to verify their identity

### 3. Suspicious Requests

Attackers may ask employees to transfer money, share credentials, open files, install software, or provide confidential information.

**Warning signs:**
- Unexpected requests
- Requests involving passwords or authentication codes
- Requests to bypass security procedures
- Requests that seem unusual for the person's role

## Key Warning Signs

Employees should be especially careful when they notice:

- Urgency or pressure
- Impersonation of trusted people
- Suspicious links or attachments
- Requests for sensitive information
- Requests to bypass normal procedures
- Unusual payment or transfer requests
- Unexpected login or verification requests

## How Employees Can Prevent Social Engineering

Employees can reduce the risk of social engineering by:

1. Verifying unexpected requests through a trusted communication channel.
2. Avoiding clicking suspicious links or opening unexpected attachments.
3. Never sharing passwords or authentication codes.
4. Following company security procedures.
5. Checking the sender's email address carefully.
6. Reporting suspicious messages to the IT or security team.
7. Taking time to verify urgent requests instead of responding immediately.

## Interview Questions

### What is social engineering?

Social engineering is the manipulation of people into performing actions or revealing information that can compromise security.

### What is protecting?

Protecting means taking appropriate security measures to prevent unauthorized access, information disclosure, fraud, and other security incidents.

### How can employees prevent social engineering?

Employees can prevent social engineering by verifying requests, protecting credentials, avoiding suspicious links and attachments, following security procedures, and reporting suspicious activity.

## Conclusion

Social engineering attacks target human behaviour rather than only technical systems. Awareness, careful verification, and following security procedures can significantly reduce the risk of successful social engineering attacks.
