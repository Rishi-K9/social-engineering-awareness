# Social Engineering Awareness Guide

## 1. Introduction

Social engineering is a form of cyber attack that targets people by manipulating their emotions, trust, or behaviour.

Attackers may pretend to be trusted individuals or organizations to convince victims to reveal information, click malicious links, transfer money, or perform other unsafe actions.

---

## 2. Common Social Engineering Scenarios

### Phishing

Phishing is an attack where a person receives a fake email or message designed to appear legitimate.

**Example:**

An employee receives an email claiming that their account will be disabled unless they click a link and verify their password.

**Warning signs:**

- Urgent language
- Suspicious links
- Unexpected attachments
- Requests for passwords
- Unusual sender addresses

**Safe action:**

Do not click the link. Verify the message through the organization's official website or a trusted contact.

---

### Impersonation

In an impersonation attack, the attacker pretends to be someone the victim trusts.

**Example:**

An attacker pretends to be an IT employee and asks an employee to provide a verification code.

**Warning signs:**

- Unexpected requests
- Pressure to act quickly
- Requests for confidential information
- Attempts to bypass normal procedures

**Safe action:**

Verify the person's identity using a trusted communication method before taking any action.

---

### Suspicious Requests

Attackers may request sensitive information, money transfers, access credentials, or other actions.

**Example:**

An employee receives a message from someone claiming to be a manager and requesting an urgent payment.

**Warning signs:**

- Unusual payment requests
- Requests for passwords or authentication codes
- Requests to bypass security procedures
- Unexpected instructions

**Safe action:**

Verify the request directly with the person through a trusted channel and follow company procedures.

---

## 3. Important Warning Signs

Employees should be cautious when they notice:

- Urgency or pressure
- Impersonation
- Suspicious requests
- Unknown or unusual senders
- Suspicious links or attachments
- Requests for sensitive information
- Requests to bypass normal procedures
- Unexpected financial requests

---

## 4. How Employees Can Protect Themselves

Employees can reduce the risk of social engineering by:

1. Verifying unexpected requests.
2. Checking email addresses and sender details.
3. Avoiding suspicious links and attachments.
4. Never sharing passwords or authentication codes.
5. Following company security procedures.
6. Reporting suspicious messages to the security or IT team.
7. Taking time to verify urgent requests instead of responding immediately.

---

## 5. Quick Safety Checklist

Before responding to an unexpected request, ask:

- Do I know the sender?
- Is the request expected?
- Is the sender creating unnecessary urgency?
- Is sensitive information being requested?
- Is there a suspicious link or attachment?
- Can I verify the request through another trusted channel?

If something feels unusual, **stop and verify before taking action.**

---

## 6. Conclusion

Social engineering attacks take advantage of human behaviour such as trust, fear, urgency, and curiosity.

Employees can reduce the risk by staying alert, verifying unusual requests, protecting sensitive information, and reporting suspicious activity.
